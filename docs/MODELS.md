# The models

Each model is a physical simulation of one material, read socially through *A Thousand Plateaus*. This page summarizes each one: the physics, what each process is in it, the lines of flight between them, and what the model adds that neither the book nor the physics supplies. The **Sources** panel in each model gives the full passages, the borrowed sources, the equations, and the divergences, and those panels are the authority where this page abbreviates. Page numbers refer to the Minnesota translation of *A Thousand Plateaus* (1987).

## Metallurgic: a world of crystals

**Physics.** A supersaturated solution on a square lattice, run by Metropolis Monte Carlo. Atoms attach and detach at random, weighted by the energy of their bonds, so small clusters keep forming and dissolving until one passes the critical size of classical nucleation theory and grows. Atoms carry one of several orientations, so a growing solid breaks into grains with boundaries between them. A finite reservoir sets the supersaturation, which growth draws down and a steady feed restores.

- **Capture: the stock.** A cluster past the threshold grows into cyan grains, drawing down what feeds it. The book speaks of an apparatus “crystallizing” (357) and of the stock that “depends on a new type of assemblage” (440).
- **War machine: smiths.** Itinerants who follow the grain boundaries and carve holes through the stock, returning matter to the solution. Smiths “follow the matter-flow” (412) and make holey space (413-15).
- **Warding off: clans.** Many small agents of varied size settle on forming clusters and block their growth, as antifreeze proteins block ice. When the stock passes an alarm level they mobilize and wear down the stock, destroying rather than keeping, since “depletion is the means by which primitive groups ward off the stock” (440).
- **Lines of flight.** A smith walled in by the stock stops carving until a gap opens (appropriation and desertion). A clan that goes long without mobilizing may seed a stock of its own (warding off hardening into a State, by nucleation on a foreign particle). A mobilized clan at a grain boundary may leave as a smith, and a smith with nothing left to carve may settle as a clan.
- **The model’s own devices.** Mobilization with an alarm level and a calm level is the model’s reading of anticipation. The lines of flight are rules, not physics, except for the seeding.

## Hydraulic: a world of liquid

**Physics.** A shallow layer of liquid flows along a channel and is heated unevenly from below, solved on a grid by Stam’s method with buoyancy (the Boussinesq approximation). Depending on the flow and the heat, the liquid runs in parallel layers (laminar flow), turns in small convection cells (Rayleigh-Bénard), or breaks into turbulence. Dye released from fixed sources at the inlet, as in Reynolds’s experiment of 1883, shows where the flow goes.

- **Capture: the corvée.** The through-flow carries each lane of dye intact toward the far edge, the State’s works. The State is forever “regulating the movement of the flow of labor, assigning it channels and conduits” (368), subordinating hydraulic force “to conduits, pipes, embankments, which prevent turbulence” (363).
- **War machine: journeymen.** Turbulence scatters the dye through the whole layer, a movement “distributed by turbulence across a smooth space” (363), like the journeymen’s compagnonnages “of the type formed by masons, carpenters, smiths” (368). Raids are hot spots that travel down the channel, breaking its lanes wherever they pass through it.
- **Warding off: households.** Small convection cells hold the dye in place ahead of the conduit’s front, each turning on its own ground, since war “maintains the dispersal and segmentarity of groups” (357).
- **Lines of flight.** Dye that crosses from one regime to another keeps a ring in the color it left: drafted labor breaking out as journeymen, households drafted, journeymen settled into guilds. The narrator reports a crossing when it runs well above its usual level.
- **The model’s own devices.** Reading the dye as labor is the model’s, drawn from page 368. The heaters drift and surge, uneven in place and time, so that the regimes take turns. On a tall screen the channel is drawn turned, so that the flow runs down the screen.

## Acoustic: a world of beats

**Physics.** A field of metronomes on one board that can sway, the classroom version of what Huygens saw in 1665 when two pendulum clocks on one beam fell into time. Each metronome is a phase oscillator (the Kuramoto model). It is pulled toward the board’s common beat in proportion to how strongly the board already swings, toward its own village, and toward its neighbors, which it hears with a small delay.

- **Capture: one bell for all.** The board’s swing pulls every metronome into one count, and each that joins makes the pull stronger. The central point “acts as a point of resonance on the horizon, behind all the other points” (224), and each State is “a redundancy of resonance (not of frequency)” (433).
- **War machine: contagion.** Neighbors pass the beat along in traveling waves, counted from no center. “Bands, human or animal, proliferate by contagion” (241), and “in a smooth space-time one occupies without counting” (477).
- **Warding off: villages.** Each village keeps its own tempo, and when the board starts to swing the villages spread their tempi wider, which raises the threshold for synchrony. Centers “do not all resonate together, they do not fall on the same point” (211).
- **Lines of flight.** A village beating faster than the field becomes a pacemaker, either hardening into a second bell that pulls its neighbors into its own count, or sending its beat outward as rings of waves. Raids are patches of strong contagion that sweep across the counted field.
- **The model’s own devices.** The villages’ spreading of their tempi is the model’s reading of anticipation. The model omits opposite-phase locking, which Huygens first saw, because the book gives the opposition of segments “two by two” to the State (223).

## Cosmic: a world of weight and speed

**Physics.** Newtonian gravity around a heavy center, integrated by leapfrog. Societies are small clusters held together by their own gravity, each with its own orbits. A heavy center’s tides strip a cluster that comes within its tidal radius. Heavy bodies passing through a crowd are slowed by the crowd’s pull (dynamical friction, after Chandrasekhar), and a heavy body falling through a dense center flings out the bodies around it.

- **Capture: the capital city.** A center that is there from the start, since the State “comes into the world fully formed” (427). Its orbits are its order. It overcodes societies by drawing them within reach of its tides, and it catches passing companies in orbit as its army: “What we call a military institution, or army, is not at all the war machine in itself, but the form under which it is appropriated by the State” (418).
- **War machine: companies.** Heavy bodies on open paths, outside every orbit. Some raid the capital city, flinging away its bodies and riding on across the field, since “the raid can be seen as another object, rather than as a particular form of war” (417). Others are slowed and caught.
- **Warding off: societies.** Each society’s tidal radius is a threshold, and when the capital city’s reach grows, threatened societies tighten their own orbits to keep that threshold outside them: “The threshold is in fact already there, but outside the limit” (440).
- **Lines of flight.** A society that tightens too far collapses into a small State of its own, which grows by drawing in what passes close and may later be annexed by the capital city. A society struck hard can throw off a group of members who ride off together as a new company.
- **The model’s own devices.** Dynamical friction is applied by its formula, since a few hundred bodies are too few for it to emerge strongly. The capital city has a halo so that even an emptied capital city draws societies toward itself. Collapse and breakaway merge bodies into one, conserving mass and momentum.

## What is not modeled yet

Polarization (the town, a circuit “defined by entries and exits,” 432) and encompassment (ecumenical organizations, which pass between formations, 435) are part of the book’s five processes but are not staged in any model.
